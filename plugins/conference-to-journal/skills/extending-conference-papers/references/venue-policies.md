# Venue policies for extended conference papers

## Contents
- How to use this table
- Publisher and journal rules
- Sources

Last checked: October 2026. Rules change, and individual journals and special issues often set their own. Treat every row as a lead to verify, not as the rule.

## How to use this table

1. Find the row for the conference paper's publisher (it controls reuse rights and sometimes sets its own extension rule) and the row for the target journal.
2. Open the target journal's current author guidelines and any special-issue call. With web search, search for the journal's name plus "extended conference paper policy". Otherwise ask the user for the link or text.
3. Record the threshold (if any), the required items, the source link, and the date checked. When the journal's own rule differs from this table, the journal's rule wins.

## Publisher and journal rules

| Publisher or venue | New material | Other requirements | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACM (all publications) | Generally at least 25% of the paper not previously published; each publication interprets this | Note prior publication on the title page if republished without substantial revision | [1] |
| ACM TISSEC (one ACM journal's author guide) | Points to ACM's 25% guideline | Full disclosure of prior publication; a brief statement explaining how the paper meets the new material requirement | [2] |
| Springer computer science proceedings (LNCS and related series) | At least 30% new material | Cite the original paper; include an explicit statement about the increment | [3] |
| IEEE (general) | No single IEEE-wide percentage; each journal or society decides | See the rows below | [4], [5], [6] |
| IEEE Communications Society journals (for example TCOM, TWC) | "Substantial" differences, judged by the editor; ComSoc describes the 30% figure as an old rule that is not IEEE or ComSoc policy | Cite the conference version in the submission system and in the paper; use an at least slightly different title; for conference papers of 8 pages or more, upload a summary of differences as a supplementary file; text and figures may be reused | [4] |
| IEEE Transactions on Learning Technologies | At least 30% (described there as the general IEEE rule); typical extensions add about 50% | Copy of the conference version and a cover letter summarizing the differences; cite the conference paper and add an acknowledgment that this is an expanded version | [5] |
| IEEE Power & Energy Society Transactions | At least 60% new for papers from PES conferences; TPWRS reviews papers with less than 40% overlap | Reference the conference paper and identify the new contributions | [6] |
| IOP Publishing (expanded computer science proceedings papers) | At least 30% new and original material | Different title and abstract; cover letter that discloses the conference paper and lists the additions | [7] |
| Wiley, Software Testing, Verification and Reliability | At least 30% new material, which must be technical contribution, not background, related work, or motivation | Cite the conference paper, discuss it, and summarize the new material; combining several conference papers without new results is not accepted | [8] |
| MDPI special issues | Set per special issue; one call for extended conference papers asked for at least 50% new material | That call also asked for a new title, abstract, and keywords | [9] |
| Elsevier | No publisher-wide rule found; set per journal | Check the journal's Guide for Authors and any special-issue call | None |

A survey of computer science journal editors found that 30% was the proportion they mentioned most often, and that nearly a quarter would not republish conference papers outside special circumstances [10].
