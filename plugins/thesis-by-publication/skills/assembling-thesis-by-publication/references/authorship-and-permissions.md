## Contribution statements

Use the university's form whenever one exists. If there is no form, draft one statement per co-authored paper with the CRediT roles below. Some forms ask for percentage contributions. Give a percentage only when the form asks for one, and only the figure the candidate supplies.

Suggest collecting signatures soon after each paper is published. Co-authors change jobs, and chasing signatures at submission time is a common delay.

The candidate states their own roles and co-authors confirm them. Present every statement as a draft for confirmation.

## CRediT roles

The Contributor Roles Taxonomy (CRediT, ANSI/NISO Z39.104-2022) has 14 roles. Each can be marked lead, equal, or supporting.

Conceptualization; Data curation; Formal analysis; Funding acquisition; Investigation; Methodology; Project administration; Resources; Software; Supervision; Validation; Visualization; Writing (original draft); Writing (review and editing).

## Statement template

```
Chapter [N]: [Full citation] ([status])

Contribution of [candidate name]:
[Role] ([lead/equal/supporting]); [Role] ([level]); ...
In plain words: [one or two sentences, for example "I designed the study,
ran all field measurements, analyzed the data, and wrote the first draft."]

Contributions of co-authors:
[Name]: [Role] ([level]); ...
[Name]: [Role] ([level]); ...

Signatures:
[Candidate] ______________ Date ________
[Co-author] ______________ Date ________
```

## Message to co-authors

```
Subject: Authorship statement for my thesis: [short paper title]

Dear [Name],

I am including our paper "[title]" ([venue], [year or status]) as Chapter [N]
of my [degree] thesis at [institution]. The university requires a statement of
each author's contribution, signed by all authors.

A draft is attached. Could you check that your contribution is described
correctly and, if you agree, sign and return it by [date]? I am glad to change
anything that is not accurate.

Thank you,
[Candidate]
```

## Permission paths

Work out the basis for including each published or accepted paper:

1. Open license (for example, CC BY). Reuse is allowed with attribution. Put the citation and license notice at the chapter start.
2. Copyright transferred, or licensed exclusively to the publisher. Read the signed agreement or the publisher's author-rights page for thesis reuse. Common conditions are a full citation, a specific credit line, using the accepted manuscript instead of the version of record in a public repository, or an embargo. Record the exact condition and its source.
3. No clear answer. Request permission through the publisher's permissions portal or by email, and keep the reply.
4. Submitted or in-preparation papers. No permission is needed yet, but check the target journal's policy on prior posting, because some journals treat a publicly deposited thesis as prior publication. If the paper is accepted before submission, update its status and repeat this check.

Check every figure or table reproduced from other authors' work as well; each needs its own permission or license.

If a publisher will not allow the paper in the public version of the thesis, the usual options are an embargo, depositing the accepted manuscript, or replacing the full text in the public copy with the citation and a link while the examination copy keeps it. Check which options the university accepts.

Record a permission as confirmed only when the user can point to its source.

## Permission request template

```
Subject: Permission to include an article in a doctoral thesis

Dear Permissions Team,

I am the [first/corresponding] author of "[title]", published in [journal],
[volume, pages, year], DOI [doi]. I would like to include this article as a
chapter of my [degree] thesis at [institution]. The thesis will be
[deposited in the university's open-access repository / held in print only].

Please let me know whether this use is permitted, which version I may include,
and any credit line I should use.

Kind regards,
[Name, affiliation, email]
```

## Chapter-start notice

Put a block like this at the start of each paper chapter, adjusted to what the rules and the publisher require:

```
This chapter is [published as / accepted for publication as / submitted as]:
[Full citation]
Version included: [version of record / accepted manuscript / submitted manuscript / reformatted for this thesis]
[Required credit line or license notice]
The contributions of each author are set out in the statement [below / in Appendix X].
```
